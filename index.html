    ID & Fake Guide

    One place to check a card before you buy or sell it: how to spot proxies and counterfeits, and how to tell look-alike editions apart (the Unlimited vs Revised kind of mistake). Pictures are the real printings from Scryfall. Tap a picture to open it bigger.

    Fake Checks - Do Them in This Order

    No single test proves a card is real. Run several, and always compare against a card you know is genuine from the same set (a common from the same era is perfect).

    1
    Compare Side by Side

    Put it next to a known-genuine card from the same set. Check size, font, letter spacing, the mana symbols, colors and how glossy it is. Fakes often have brighter colors, lost detail in the art, or slightly different fonts (the type line and the blue mana drop are common giveaways).

    2
    Loupe Test (Most Reliable)

    Under a 30-60x loupe, the colors on a real card are a neat flower-like rosette of dots. The black text, frame and mana-symbol outlines are printed as solid, sharp lines on top. On fakes the dots are blurry or chaotic, and black lines are made of dots or have fuzzy edges. Straight lines (like the box around "Deckmaster" on the back) should be perfectly straight.

    3
    Weigh It

    A real card weighs about 1.7-1.8 g. Many recent fakes weigh about 1.9 g because of extra plastic. Use a 0.01 g scale and weigh a known-genuine card from the same era first: anything around 1.85 g or more deserves a closer look. Rebacks (a front glued onto a real back) are usually heavier too.

    4
    Light Test

    Shine a bright LED through the card. Real cards glow with a slight blue tint from the blue core layer, which you can also see as a thin line on the edge. A black core or a card that blocks the light is a big red flag. Some fakes pass this test, and a few genuine sets (Ice Age, for example) have no blue core, so compare with a real card from the same set.

    5
    Check the Back

    Three details fakes usually get wrong: the yellow spot inside the green dot has four tiny red dots in an L shape with a solid black ring around it; the "T" in "The" has a straight left edge and wavy bottom and right edges; and there are tiny "demon horn" crescents above each "E" in Deckmaster.

    6
    Modern Rares: Holo Stamp

    Rares and mythics since Core Set 2015 (2014) have an oval holographic stamp at the bottom center. Fake stamps often look flat or damaged up close.

    Skip these: the bend test (it can crease a real card), the rip test (destroys it), and the blacklight test (unreliable). Graded slabs: always look up the certificate number on the grader's own website - fake slabs and labels exist.

    Tools Worth Owning

    ⚖️ 0.01 g Pocket ScaleFor the weight test. Cheap and fast.
    🔍 30-60x LoupeFor the rosette and black-line checks. The single most useful tool.
    🔦 Bright LED LightFor the light test (a phone torch works).
    📐 Corner Radius GaugeFor Alpha vs Beta: Alpha corners are about 2 mm, Beta and later about 1 mm.
    🃏 Known-Genuine CardsA few commons from each era you buy, to compare against.

    Not-Legal Versions That Look Like the Real Thing

    These are genuine Wizards products, but worth far less than the originals and not legal in tournaments. They are often listed in ways that make them look like Alpha or Beta.

    Listing Red Flags

    • Words in the title or description: proxy, custom, altered, playtest, "reprint", "replica", "not tournament legal", "fan made", "MPC". The scanner already skips these.
    • Too cheap: far under what the card normally sells for - the "too cheap?" tag on eBay Deals.
    • Stock photos or one blurry photo, or photos that never show the back.
    • Edition words that don't match the photos: "Beta" with white borders, "Unlimited" with a tap symbol, "Legends" with a white border.
    • No condition stated on an expensive card, or damage words hidden in the description (the scanner reads the full description).
    • Low-feedback or brand-new sellers on high-value cards.
    • Graded slabs without a clear photo of the label and certificate number.

    Auto-Track Lists

    Instead of adding cards one at a time, each list is a Scryfall search. Every card it matches gets priced daily (shown in All prices) and scanned for eBay deals, using the list's drop % and deal % alerts. Use "Preview" to see what a search matches before saving.

    Example searches
    • is:reserved usd>=50: Reserved List cards worth $50+
    • set:arn usd>=100: Arabian Nights cards worth $100+
    • (set:lea or set:leb) t:artifact: Alpha and Beta artifacts
    • set:3ed is:dual: Revised dual lands
